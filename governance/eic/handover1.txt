Human Clarification — EIC Adversarial Unit / Multi-Account Interpretation
Before any further EIC shaping, I want to clarify the intended meaning of the participant-layer requirement and HD-EIC-03.
Question:
Does PIL-003 require that a sufficiently large multi-account player or organisation must be unable to internalise all economically important professions, or does PIL-003 instead require that meaningful comparative advantage, specialisation, trade, and opportunity for interaction remain economically valuable even when legitimate multi-accounting, multiple characters, organisations, and broad multi-profession capability are allowed?

My intended design direction is the second interpretation.
TCIndustries is not intended to prevent a player from:
- having multiple characters/accounts;
- multi-boxing;
- developing multiple professions;
- operating businesses or organisations;
- vertically integrating production chains;
- becoming highly economically capable.
These capabilities are legitimate parts of the sandbox and are not themselves design failures.
The failure condition should therefore not be:
"A sufficiently organised player can perform the entire production chain."

The relevant failure condition should instead be:
"Even though a player, multi-account participant, or organisation can become broadly capable and vertically integrated, the economic system fails to preserve meaningful comparative advantage and economically attractive reasons for independent participants to specialise, trade, cooperate, or provide services to one another."

Requested ruling
Please treat this as a clarification of the interpretation of the existing requirement, not as permission to invent a new architecture or residual mechanism.
HD-EIC-09 — Multi-Account / Broad-Capability Interpretation
HUMAN-LOCKED INTENT:
Legitimate multi-accounting, multi-boxing, multiple characters, broad profession ownership, and organisational vertical integration are permitted and are not themselves failures of PIL-003. The EIC requirement is concerned with whether meaningful comparative advantage and economically valuable interdependence/opportunity can persist despite that capability.

Therefore:
- Do not introduce character-count restrictions.
- Do not introduce account restrictions.
- Do not require professions to be mutually exclusive.
- Do not require a player to remain economically dependent merely because they lack access to another profession.
- Do not treat successful vertical integration by itself as falsification.
- Do not begin new architecture generation yet.
- Do not invent residual channels yet.
First, reconcile the existing EIC evidence and simulation specification against this clarification and determine whether the previous Architecture C falsification remains valid under the intended interpretation, or whether the adversarial test was testing a stronger condition than PIL-003 actually requires.
