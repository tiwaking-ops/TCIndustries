## GATE 1 — Unit of Interdependence

**Question**  
What is the primary unit that the EIC is trying to keep from efficiently internalising every economically important role?

**Why the simulation cannot answer it**  
The simulation can only test the unit it is given. Candidate v0.1 primarily constrains characters (Layer 1). The falsification and simulation showed that player-level (Layer 2) and economic-actor-level (Layer 3) internalisation are the harder and more consequential attacks. Choosing the unit is a statement of project intent, not an empirical result.

**Available options**

| Option | Statement |
|---|---|
| **A — Character-level** | A single character should not be able to efficiently perform every economically important role. |
| **B — Player-level** | A single player should not be able to efficiently internalise every economically important role (including via multiple characters). |
| **C — Economic-actor-level** | No rational economic actor (individual, multi-account player, or organisation) should be able to efficiently internalise every economically important stage. |
| **D — Layered** | Character specialisation is desirable as identity/progression, but the binding invariant is player/economic-actor comparative advantage rather than character inability. |

**Consequences of each**

- **A** → Formal concentration budget becomes a natural instrument; multi-account and organisational closure remain largely unaddressed by M1.  
- **B** → Multi-account policy becomes central; character-level restrictions alone are insufficient.  
- **C** → Strongest and hardest target; requires mechanisms that remain effective against organisations, not only characters or accounts.  
- **D** → Character specialisation retained for identity fantasy; primary proof of interdependence shifts to comparative advantage, ETA, and resistance to full internalisation at player/actor level.

**What becomes testable afterward**  
Whether the chosen unit’s invariant can be sustained under the adversarial agents already defined (multi-account, organisation, factory, optimiser).

**What remains TBD**  
Exact instruments (budget form, facility rules, ownership rules, etc.).

**Recommendation (non-binding):** **D — Layered.**  
Preserves the citizen/profession fantasy and PIL-003’s “opportunity rather than coercion” requirement, while recognising that the economically meaningful invariant is at the player/actor level. Consistent with the simulation’s finding that Layer 1 restrictions do not automatically solve Layers 2 and 3.

## GATE 2 — Specialist Value Definition

**Question**  
What category (or categories) of value must specialists retain even if industrial production can match raw measured quality?

**Why the simulation cannot answer it**  
Test A could not discriminate residual survival because the Candidate lists residual channels but does not state which category of value is intended to be load-bearing. Without that intent, any residual model is guesswork.

**Available options** (combinable; not mutually exclusive)

| Category | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Performance** | Specialist produces strictly better measured goods |
| **Economic** | Specialist produces at lower total cost (including attention and risk) |
| **Information** | Specialist knows what to make, when, and from which resources |
| **Customisation** | Specialist provides economically non-standardised outcomes |
| **Responsiveness** | Specialist reacts to changing conditions faster than industrial processes |
| **Identity / Provenance** | Consumers value who made the thing |
| **Service** | Specialist provides associated services that pure production does not |
| **Discovery** | Specialist finds or creates new opportunities |

**Consequences**

- Relying primarily on **Performance** while allowing factory quality parity creates a direct contradiction unless performance is redefined.  
- Relying on **Identity / Provenance** or **Customisation** requires consumers to actually value those dimensions; the simulation showed outcomes are sensitive to this preference weight.  
- Relying on **Information**, **Responsiveness**, or **Discovery** shifts specialist value toward knowledge and timing rather than unit production.  
- Relying on **Service** couples production to ongoing relationships.

**What becomes testable afterward**  
Whether the chosen value categories survive progressive industrialisation and optimiser attack (a properly specified Test A).

**What remains TBD**  
Exact mechanics that implement the chosen categories; relative weights; whether any category is allowed to be the sole load-bearing one.

**Recommendation (non-binding):**  
Prioritise a **portfolio** rather than a single category: Information + Responsiveness + Identity/Provenance + Customisation, with Performance *not* required to remain permanently exclusive to specialists under factory parity. Explicitly reject dependence on an arbitrary hard factory-quality ceiling. Final mix is a human preference about the kind of world TCIndustries is.

## GATE 3 — Multi-Accounting Stance

**Question**  
What is the project’s intended stance toward multi-account economic integration?

**Why the simulation cannot answer it**  
Multi-account efficiency is undefined. The simulation showed that Soft vs Formal outcomes diverge sharply only when that efficiency is high. Whether high efficiency is “in scope” as a legitimate player strategy is a design philosophy choice, not a simulation output. OQ-012 remains open in the GDD.

**Available options**

| Option | Stance |
|---|---|
| **M1 — Legitimate and in-scope** | The system should remain economically coherent even if one player operates several characters. Hardest target; strongest form of player-level interdependence. |
| **M2 — Allowed but not required to preserve identical economics** | Multi-accounting is permitted; highly committed players may internalise more; the game primarily balances around ordinary single-account and small-group interaction. |
| **M3 — Certain forms deliberately constrained** | Specific multi-account economic integration is limited (account-level systems, ownership rules, simultaneous-operation limits, or similar). |

**Consequences**

- **M1** → Every M1/M4 mechanism must be evaluated against efficient multi-account optimisers; character-level restrictions alone are insufficient.  
- **M2** → Softens the proof burden; multi-account advantage becomes an accepted gradient rather than a failure condition.  
- **M3** → Introduces explicit anti-multi-account instruments; higher design and policy cost; may conflict with sandbox freedom preferences.

**What becomes testable afterward**  
Which concentration and residual mechanisms remain effective under the chosen stance; whether ETA stays in a healthy band for ordinary players.

**What remains TBD**  
Exact instruments if M3 is chosen; exact efficiency assumptions if M1 or M2 is chosen.

**Recommendation (non-binding):** **M1 or M2**, with a preference for clarifying intent before inventing constraints. M3 should not be defaulted into. The simulation already showed that treating multi-accounting as an afterthought makes the EIC look successful at Layer 1 while failing at Layer 2.

## GATE 4 — Provisional Functional Shapes

**Question**  
Are we willing to authorise provisional functional shapes (not numbers) for the under-specified relationships so that a subsequent simulation can discriminate architecture success from failure?

**Why the simulation cannot answer it**  
This is a methodological decision about how the project proceeds. BAL-001 forbids premature numeric tuning; it does not forbid structural modelling. The minimum simulation hit the boundary of what labels alone can test.

**Available options**

| Option | Stance |
|---|---|
| **S1 — Yes, provisional shapes only** | Authorise non-canonical functional forms (curves, relationships, qualitative dynamics) sufficient to re-run discriminating tests. Numbers remain illustrative or deferred. |
| **S2 — No, remain at concept level** | Keep M1/M4 as labelled concepts; accept that architecture-level empirical discrimination is deferred until later design phases. |
| **S3 — Partial** | Authorise shapes only for the relationships that Gates 1–3 make load-bearing; leave the rest conceptual. |

**Consequences**

- **S1** → Enables a second simulation that can return Architecture PASS/FAIL rather than UNDER-SPECIFIED.  
- **S2** → Preserves maximum openness; leaves Architecture C underdetermined indefinitely.  
- **S3** → Focused progress; still requires Gates 1–3 first.

**What becomes testable afterward**  
Under S1 or S3: properly specified Test A and Test B.  
Under S2: nothing further at the architecture-evidence level until design advances by other means.

**What remains TBD**  
All numeric values; final mechanical implementations.

**Recommendation (non-binding):** **S3 — Partial**, contingent on Gates 1–3. Authorise only the functional shapes required by the chosen unit of interdependence, specialist value categories, and multi-account stance. Do not authorise a full numeric model.
