## HD-EIC-06 — Status of the EIC Requirement Itself

**Question:**  
Does the canonical requirement remain unchanged?

The locked statement is:  
> Important professions should depend upon one another, while interdependence must create opportunity rather than coercive inconvenience.

The failed experiment demonstrates that the mechanisms tested do not reliably produce that property at the independent-participant layer (the validation unit required by HD-EIC-01).

**Possible rulings:**

| Option | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **REQUIREMENT UNCHANGED** | PIL-003 and the participant-layer interpretation remain binding. A viable mechanism must still be found or the requirement must be explicitly revised later. |
| **REQUIREMENT NARROWED** | Human revises the interpretation of interdependence (e.g., character-layer interdependence is accepted as sufficient, or multi-account/organisational internalisation is no longer treated as a first-class adversarial condition). |
| **REQUIREMENT RELAXED OR DEFERRED** | The strength or timing of the interdependence requirement is reduced. |

**Recommendation:** REQUIREMENT UNCHANGED.  
Changing the pillar because the current mechanism failed would invert the governance order. The pillar constrains the mechanism, not the reverse.

# 5. Architectural Observation (Non-Binding)

The experimental programme demonstrated that repeatedly adding or refining soft residual channels (attention load, customisation, process knowledge, discovery, locality, facility concentration, information maintenance) does not, under the tested formulations, prevent a rational multi-account or organisational participant from driving independent-specialist ETA to ≤ 0 once quality parity, NPC baselines, and demand neutralisation are applied.

This suggests that any future viable architecture will likely require one or more structural commitments that are stronger than "soft residual advantage that organisations can also acquire." Possible categories of stronger commitment (listed only as observation, not as proposals) include:

- Genuine non-internalisable scarcity of some production factor.  
- Persistent information or discovery asymmetry that scale does not cheaply erase.  
- Demand structures that remain differentiated even after capability rises.  
- Institutional or social constraints on full internalisation that are not purely economic.

Whether the project is willing to make any such commitment is a human design decision, not a simulation output.
