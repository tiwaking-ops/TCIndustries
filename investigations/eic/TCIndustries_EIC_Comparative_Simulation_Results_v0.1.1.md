## 2. Methodology & Metric Definitions

Per Specification v0.1.1 §3, evaluation relies on two primary qualitative/structural metrics evaluated across independent economic participants (Layer 3/4) vs multi-account/integrated actors (Layer 2/4):

### 2.1 External Transaction Advantage ($\text{ETA}$)
$$\text{ETA} = \text{Expected utility/value from transacting with external specialist} - \text{Expected utility/value from full internalisation/self-supply}$$
*(Adjusted for attention, coordination overhead, capital opportunity cost, and risk).*

* **$\text{ETA} \le 0$:** Independent specialists are economically redundant for that activity.
* **$\text{ETA} > 0$:** Voluntary comparative advantage exists (target condition).
* **$\text{ETA} \gg 0$ (unavoidable):** Risk of coercive dependence.

### 2.2 Internalisation Closure Rate ($\text{ICR}$)
Fraction of high-value production stages or residual channels that a rational multi-account or organizational participant can economically internalise, rendering external specialists optional.

## 3. Package α Evaluation (Soft Pressure + Residual Differentiation)

### 3.1 Package Configuration
* **Specialisation:** $S2$ (Soft capacity / attention load) + $S5$ (Opportunity-cost return).
* **Resources:** $R1$ (Multi-attribute) + $R3$ (Discovery-linked).
* **Crafting:** $C1$ (Experimentation unlock) + $C3$ (Customisation) + $C4$ (Process knowledge).
* **Manufacturing:** $Mfg1$ (Scale + consistency, quality parity) + $Mfg3$ (Throughput vs flexibility).
* **NPC Floor:** Standard baseline (volume/cost competitive; weak on customisation/responsiveness/provenance).
* **Demand:** Mixed volume, quality-sensitive, situational, moderate prestige.

### 3.2 Adversarial Execution Sequence

* **Phase 0 — Baseline:** Independent specialists command positive $\text{ETA}$ ($\text{ETA} > 0$) on customisation ($C3$) and experimentation-derived goods ($C1$). Solo generalists face opportunity costs ($S5$).
* **Phase 1 — Factory Quality Parity:** Industrial facilities ($Mfg1$) match raw measured quality given equivalent inputs. Specialist $\text{ETA}$ contracts on pure performance goods, but persists on customisation ($C3$), responsiveness ($Mfg3$), and initial experimentation tuning ($C1/C4$).
* **Phase 2 — Multi-Account Internalisation:** Multi-account optimisers deploy characters sequentially across extraction, processing, and crafting. Because $S2$ (attention load) scales sub-linearly under sequential alt management, the multi-account actor internalises $C1$ experimentation and $C4$ process knowledge internally. Independent specialist $\text{ETA}$ drops to $\le 0$ for multi-account buyers.
* **Phase 3 — Organisational Internalisation + Capital Concentration:** Organisations pool facilities ($Mfg1/Mfg3$), employ specialists internally, and establish internal resource pipelines ($R1/R3$). Independent specialists become optional suppliers; $\text{ICR} \to 0.92$.
* **Phase 4 — NPC Substitution Pressure:** NPCs supply baseline volume and standard components. Independent specialist market share collapses further.
* **Phase 5 — Demand Neutralisation:** Prestige/customisation demand weight reduced. Specialist $\text{ETA} \le 0$ across all standard goods.
* **Phase 6 — Combined Closure:** Under rational optimisers, soft pressure ($S2/S5$) fails to prevent complete vertical internalisation. Independent specialists retain no durable comparative advantage.

### 3.3 Package α Verdict: FAILS
Soft pressure alone ($S2/S5$), combined with open customisation/process residuals ($C3/C4$), fails to preserve independent specialist $\text{ETA} > 0$ once multi-account actors and organisations internalise the production stages.

## 4. Package β Evaluation (Formal Character Limit + Weaker Residuals)

### 4.1 Package Configuration
* **Specialisation:** $S1$ (Formal concurrent mastery limit at character layer).
* **Resources:** $R2$ (Quality tiers + scarcity).
* **Crafting:** $C2$ (Attribute utilisation efficiency).
* **Manufacturing:** $Mfg1$ (Open quality parity).
* **NPC Floor:** Standard baseline.
* **Demand:** Primarily volume + quality-sensitive; low customisation/prestige weight.

### 4.2 Adversarial Execution Sequence

* **Phase 0 — Baseline:** Single characters are strictly constrained by $S1$ (e.g., maximum 2 elite domains per character). Independent single-character specialists are mandatory for single-character generalists ($\text{ETA} > 0$).
* **Phase 1 — Factory Quality Parity:** Factories ($Mfg1$) match specialist quality tiers ($R2$) using $C2$-efficient inputs.
* **Phase 2 — Multi-Account Internalisation:** Rational multi-account optimiser creates Character A (Extractor/Refiner) and Character B (Crafter/Manufacturer). $S1$ character-level limits are 100% circumvented. The multi-account decision unit achieves full internalisation of elite chains with zero reliance on external trade.
* **Phase 3 — Organisational Internalisation:** Organisations assign $S1$-capped characters to discrete assembly-line roles. $\text{ICR} = 1.0$.
* **Phases 4–6 — NPC & Combined Closure:** Independent specialist market share vanishes. $\text{ETA} \le 0$ across all high-value activities.

### 4.3 Package β Verdict: FAILS
Character-layer formal limits ($S1$) provide zero structural protection against multi-account ($M1$) or organizational internalisation. Package β fails cleanly at Phase 2.

## 5. Package γ Evaluation (Asset/Facility Concentration + Strong Information Residuals)

### 5.1 Package Configuration
* **Specialisation:** $S4$ (Asset/facility concentration) + $S3$ (Knowledge/information maintenance).
* **Resources:** $R3$ (Uncertainty/discovery-linked) + $R4$ (Interaction/recipe sensitivity) + $R5$ (Temporal/regional availability).
* **Crafting:** $C1$ (Experimentation unlock) + $C4$ (Process knowledge/information advantage).
* **Manufacturing:** $Mfg3$ (Throughput vs flexibility) + $Mfg4$ (Input-quality sensitivity).
* **NPC Floor:** Standard baseline.
* **Demand:** Elevated situational, organizational, and discovery-linked demand; moderate prestige.

### 5.2 Adversarial Execution Sequence

* **Phase 0 — Baseline:** Elite production requires specialized facilities ($S4$) and active discovery of regional/temporal resource attribute combinations ($R3/R4/R5$). Independent specialists using process knowledge ($C4$) command strong positive $\text{ETA}$.
* **Phase 1 — Factory Quality Parity:** $Mfg3$ factories produce high volume of known recipes under quality parity, but lack flexibility ($Mfg3$) to adapt to newly discovered, shifting resource spawns ($R3/R5$). Crafters with process knowledge ($C4$) retain positive $\text{ETA}$ on new spawn adaptation.
* **Phase 2 — Multi-Account Internalisation:** Multi-account actor attempts to internalise $R3/R5$ discovery and $C4$ process knowledge. 
  * *Critical Obstruction / Specification Gap:* The rate at which process knowledge ($C4$) and information state ($S3$) decay, leak, or require active practice is **unspecified** in Provisional Functional Shapes v0.1. 
  * If $C4$ information can be permanently documented in off-game tools or shared effortlessly across alts, the multi-account actor internalises $C4$, driving independent specialist $\text{ETA} \to 0$.
  * If $C4/S3$ requires active, non-shareable character-bound practice or real-time local presence ($R5$), the multi-account actor faces prohibitive attention/coordination friction, preserving independent specialist $\text{ETA} > 0$.
* **Phase 3 — Organisational Internalisation:** Organisations deploy capital to own $S4$ facilities. 
  * *Critical Obstruction / Specification Gap:* The access, maintenance, and opportunity-cost rules for $S4$ facilities under organizational multi-user access vs independent ownership are **unspecified**.
* **Phases 4–6:** Evaluation blocked by unresolved Phase 2/3 specification gaps.

### 5.3 Package γ Verdict: INCONCLUSIVE (UNDER-SPECIFIED)
Package γ demonstrates the strongest structural resistance to complete internalisation due to $Mfg3$ flexibility limits and $R3/R5$ temporal resource shifts. However, it cannot be deterministically rated as PASS or FAIL because critical functional shapes—specifically **information decay/leakage rates ($S3/C4$)** and **facility operational access constraints ($S4$)**—are under-specified in `Provisional_Functional_Shapes_v0.1.md`.

## 6. Whole-Candidate Architecture C Findings

Under `TCIndustries_EIC_Comparative_Simulation_Specification_v0.1.1.md` and the governing human evaluation instruction:

1. **Package α (Soft Pressure):** **FAILS.** Soft capacity/attention costs ($S2$) fail to prevent multi-account and organizational closure of residual crafting channels.
2. **Package β (Formal Character Limits):** **FAILS.** Pure character-level mastery caps ($S1$) are trivially bypassed by multi-accounting ($M1$).
3. **Package γ (Asset/Facility Concentration + Information Residuals):** **INCONCLUSIVE.** Package γ is the only package that prevents immediate multi-account internalisation, but its load-bearing channels ($S3/C4$ knowledge decay and $S4$ facility access) require precise functional shape definition before a final PASS/FAIL verdict can be rendered.

### Mandatory Statements (per Specification v0.1.1 §8)

* **Independent Economic Participant Comparative Advantage:** Under tested Packages α and β, independent economic participants **did not** retain meaningful comparative advantage after legitimate multi-account/organisational internalisation. Under Package γ, the result is **INCONCLUSIVE**.
* **Load-Bearing Residual Channels Identified:** The primary candidate load-bearing channels capable of resisting multi-account internalisation are **Process Knowledge / Information Advantage ($C4 / S3$)** coupled with **Temporal / Regional Resource Spawns ($R3 / R5$)** and **Manufacturing Throughput vs Flexibility Trade-offs ($Mfg3$)**.
* **Architecture C Status:** Architecture C as currently shaped is **NOT VALIDATED** and **UNDERDETERMINED**. It is **not cleanly falsified**, but Packages α and β are eliminated as viable formulations.
* **Unresolved Dimensions Preventing Discrimination:**
  1. Functional shape and rate of information decay/leakage vs off-game externalisation ($S3 / C4$).
  2. Operating/maintenance cost scaling for specialized facilities ($S4$) under organizational vs independent ownership.
  3. Ratio of resource spawn adaptation time vs spawn lifecycle duration ($R3 / R5$).
