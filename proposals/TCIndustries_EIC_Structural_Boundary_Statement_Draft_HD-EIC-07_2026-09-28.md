# 1. The outstanding decision

> **What structural source(s) of economic comparative advantage is the project
> willing to make a durable design commitment to?**

Three qualifiers carry the weight (paraphrase of the gate's framing, not an
owner ruling):

- **Structural** — a property of the world's construction, not a soft nudge,
  friction tax, or convenience cost.
- **Source** — a *category* of advantage, not a specific mechanism. This
  statement names no mechanism.
- **Durable** — survives concentration, multi-accounting, and organisational
  pooling. "Durable" is precisely what the four failed packages were not.

The gate is explicit that this is a human design question and not something an
agent may derive: *"Whether the project is willing to make any such commitment
is a human design decision, not a simulation output."* (`..._Validation_Gate_..._v0.1.md:213`)

# 3. SLOT 2 — The structural commitments (the core of the statement)

The gate's §5 observation, quoted: *"any future viable architecture will likely
require one or more structural commitments that are stronger than 'soft residual
advantage that organisations can also acquire.'*"

`[[OWNER SET: COMMITMENTS]]` — the owner may adopt one, several, or none (replacing
the set with a boundary of their own naming). For each adopted category, the
owner should state **what is committed** in structural terms and **what remains
undecided**.

### Category 1 — Non-internalisable scarcity

> Gate wording: *"Genuine non-internalisable scarcity of some production factor."*

- **What commitment would mean:** some production input is scarce in a way capital, alt-count, and organisational pooling cannot cover.
- **What the record shows:** every tested package's scarcity was *purchasable*. γ′-1 tested active scarcity and it held at Phase 2 ("reduced but positive") but failed at Phase 6 — coverage cost was real but insufficient (`..._Retest_Results_v0.1.md:60, 64-68`). Under G1 (the reconciliation's reading), this category is **closed** — all tested scarcity was ultimately purchasable. Under G2, re-opening may be possible.
- **Would bound:** the whole world-construction layer — where resources exist, who may access them, and what "ownership" of a scarce thing means. This is the broadest-reaching of the four; it constrains GDD §8–9 world structure, not just economy.
- **Would not authorise:** any specific resource, placement rule, or access mechanism.
- Owner note: `[[OWNER: …]]`

### Category 2 — Persistent information/discovery asymmetry

> Gate wording: *"Persistent information or discovery asymmetry that scale does not cheaply erase."*

- **What commitment would mean:** some knowledge advantage survives an organisation fielding its own discovery and knowledge-maintenance apparatus.
- **What the record shows:** this is the channel the evidence identified as *most load-bearing* before any test ran — γ's C4 process knowledge and R3/R5 temporal-regional spawns were the candidates that kept γ INCONCLUSIVE rather than failed (`..._Results_v0.1.1.md:137`). It was then killed twice: γ′-1 because organisations maintain their own discovery agents, γ′-2 because they site facilities and keep updaters (`..._Retest_Results_v0.1.md:68, 86`). Under G1 (reconciliation's reading), this category is effectively **closed** — all tested knowledge advantages were organisation-maintainable. Under G2, re-opening may be possible.
- **Would bound:** the design must contain a channel where *scale is the counter-weight* — larger organisations face progressively diminishing returns on discovery investment, not zero-cost parity.
- **Would not authorise:** any specific discovery surface, knowledge format, or codification mechanism; no numeric parameter; no provenance/reputation engine (DEFERRED, HD-EIC-08).
- Owner note: `[[OWNER: …]]`

### Category 3 — Demand structures that remain differentiated even after capability rises

> Gate wording implicitly: *"Demand structures that remain differentiated even after capability rises."*

- **What commitment would mean:** some form of demand differentiation survives even as player/organisation capability grows — i.e., better capabilities do not erase the advantage of certain demand configurations.
- **What the record shows:** The evidence did not fully test this channel. Phase 5's stated purpose was testing whether asserted residuals are load-bearing (spec L188-191) under progressive demand-neutralisation. The reconciliation flags partial demand-neutralisation was legitimately in the bar (§6.1), and the survival bar explicitly included "under combined pressure" including partial demand neutralisation. No package tested demonstrated that demand differentiation survives capability rise *independently* of internalisation effects.
- **Would bound:** any future architecture must contain demand-configuration channels that cannot be neutralised by capability growth alone — e.g., demand that is fundamentally different in kind, not just intensity.
- **Would not authorise:** any numeric demand model; no new residual channels; no provenance/reputation engine (DEFERRED).
- Owner note: `[[OWNER: …]]`

### Category 4 — Institutional or social constraints on full internalisation that are not purely economic

> Gate wording: *"Institutional or social constraints on full internalisation that are not purely economic."*

- **What commitment would mean:** governance structures, social norms, or institutional arrangements that constrain full economic internalisation beyond what markets or organisational pooling can achieve.
- **What the record shows:** HD-EIC-03 explicitly authorised legitimate multi-accounting as in-scope adversarial condition but stated the system need not equalise multi-account and independent-player economics, but multi-account internalisation must not trivially eliminate economically meaningful independent participation. This is the closest the record comes to a non-economic constraint — multi-accounting is permitted but must not trivially eliminate. However, the evidence tested *economic* internalisation mechanisms (quality parity, NPC baselines, demand neutralisation) rather than institutional/social ones per se.
- **Would bound:** any future architecture must include governance or social constraints that are not reducible to economic incentives — e.g., governance rules that limit pooling, social norms against certain internalisation patterns, institutional arrangements with non-economic enforcement mechanisms.
- **Would not authorise:** any specific institutional design; no numeric parameter; no provenance/reputation engine (DEFERRED); no GDD consolidation (NOT AUTHORISED).
- Owner note: `[[OWNER: …]]`
